About CybICS
What this lab is, how it's deployed, and who it's for
Purpose

CybICS is an open-source ICS security training platform: a complete industrial facility with a physical process, PLC, HMI, gateways and an IDS. You attack it, detect the attacks and defend it in hands-on exercises.

Physical & virtual

Run the full lab either way:

  • Physical — Raspberry Pi + a custom CybICS PCB (STM32) running the physical process of the facility, for hardware-level attacks (UART, SWD).
  • Virtual — the same stack fully containerized with Docker Compose, ready in minutes on any OS.
Who it's for

Open to anyone curious about ICS/OT cybersecurity, from students to seasoned defenders. Aligned with IEC 62443, you investigate cybersecurity from an Operator's perspective.

Goal: hands-on cybersecurity knowledge, not just theory.

Physical Process

The plant moves gas from a Gas Storage Tank (GST) into a High Pressure Tank (HPT). The PLC switches a supply signal, a compressor and a system valve to keep the HPT in its target band. The numbers below come from the PLC program (cybICS.st) and the process model.

What the PLC aims for

ActuatorRule in automatic mode
GST supplyOn when GST falls below 60 bar, off at 240 bar
CompressorOn when HPT falls below 60 bar, off at 90 bar; only while GST is above 50 bar
System valveOpen only while HPT is between 50 and 100 bar

So in normal operation the GST sits near the top of its range and the HPT moves between 60 and 90 bar.

What the board's LEDs show

The LEDs (and the virtual hardware view) show bands, not the control targets above.

TankBand (bar)LED
GSTbelow 50 / 50–149 / 150 and aboveLow / Normal / Full
HPT0 / 1–49 / 50–99 / 100–149 / 150 and aboveEmpty / Low / Normal / High / Critical
SystemHPT between 50 and 100 with the system valve openWorking (otherwise Error)

A full GST is therefore normal. HPT "Critical" means far above target; it does not yet open the blow-out valve.

Safety: blow-out valve (BO)

A mechanical relief valve, not controlled by the PLC. It opens above 220 bar and stays open, venting gas, until the HPT is back at 200 bar.

Security implication: an attacker who drives the HPT past 220 bar forces a gas release. The Theory Path lesson shows how.
