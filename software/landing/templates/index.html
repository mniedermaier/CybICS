Step 01 · Orientation
About CybICS
What this lab is, how it's deployed, and who it's for
Purpose

CybICS is an open-source training platform that helps cybersecurity professionals, students, and researchers understand the unique challenges of securing industrial control systems (ICS) and SCADA environments through realistic, hands-on exercises.

Physical & virtual

Run the full lab either way:

  • Physical — Raspberry Pi + a custom CybICS PCB (STM32) driving a real gas-pressure process, for hardware-level attacks (UART, SWD).
  • Virtual — the same stack fully containerized with Docker Compose, ready in minutes on any OS.
Who it's for

Open to anyone curious about ICS/OT cybersecurity, from students to seasoned defenders. Aligned with IEC 62443, you investigate cybersecurity from an Operator's perspective.

Goal: hands-on cybersecurity knowledge, not just theory.

Physical Process

CybICS simulates a gas pressure control system commonly found in industrial environments. This simple yet realistic process provides an excellent foundation for learning ICS cybersecurity concepts.

System overview

The system maintains gas pressure in a High Pressure Tank (HPT) using gas from a Gas Storage Tank (GST). A PLC controls a compressor that transfers gas between tanks while monitoring pressure levels and safety conditions.

Gas Storage Tank (GST)

Buffer tank for the external gas supply. The PLC maintains GST pressure between 60–240 bar.

Pressure (bar)StatusIndicator
< 50LowRed
50 – 149NormalGreen
150+FullBlue

Control loop: below 60 bar the PLC starts filling from the external supply until GST reaches 240 bar; filling is prevented when GST is too low, to protect the compressor.

High Pressure Tank (HPT)

Main buffer tank providing pressure to the system. Target range: 60–90 bar.

Pressure (bar)StatusMeaning
0EmptySystem offline
1 – 49LowBelow operating range
50 – 99NormalSafe operating range
100 – 149HighAbove target, but safe
150+CriticalDangerous overpressure

Control loop: the compressor starts when HPT drops below 60 bar (and GST is above 50 bar), and stops once HPT reaches 90 bar. It is disabled entirely if GST falls below 50 bar, as a safety interlock.

System operation

The system operates normally while HPT stays between 50–100 bar. Below 50 bar there isn't enough pressure to run; above 100 bar the system is at risk of damage.

Safety: blowout valve (BO)

A mechanical safety valve — not PLC-controlled — prevents catastrophic failure: it opens above 220 bar to vent toxic gas to atmosphere, and closes again once HPT drops below 200 bar.

Security implication: triggering the blowout releases toxic gas.
